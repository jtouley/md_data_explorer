# ADR015: True numbers on screen; small counts masked only where they leave the machine

## Status

Accepted (2026-11-20), by the repository owner's decision.

**Supersedes:** every small-cell and derivability rule for what the app shows on screen, in
particular:

- ADR013 §4.3 (suppression on the SQL path), §4.4's differencing row, §4.5 (per-answer filter
  check: the 2^k cells and `MAX_CONDITIONS`), §11.3's definition of LEAK and of "correctly held"
  for small cells, and §12's "The log of shown counts (as built)" in full;
- ADR014 §7's framing of declared estimates as an *exception* to an app-wide small-cell rule:
  the "trust the source" handling itself is unchanged, but there is no longer a rule for it to be
  an exception to;
- the small-cell parts of `docs/vision/PURPOSE.md`, `SECURITY.md`,
  `docs/security/THREAT_MODEL.md` and `docs/app/DESIGN.md`, updated in the same change.

Not superseded: everything in ADR013 and ADR014 about correctness (grain, units, word
accounting, holds, the estimate-table traps), and the local-only design.

## Context

The app is a local clinical analytics tool. A physician asks plain-English questions over their
own research data, which already sits on their own machine.

Over ADR013 and the PRs that built it (#106, #138, #142, #143, #144, #146), the app accumulated
rules that withheld an answer so that a count of 1 to 10 patients could not be shown or worked
out from what was shown:

- per-answer small-cell suppression with secondary (complementary) suppression and folding of
  small groups into "Other" (`result_presentation._suppress_lines`, `_Lines`, `_fold_rows`);
- derivability and differencing checks within an answer (2^k condition cells on the governed SQL
  path) and across answers (the per-dataset log of shown counts with its SVD and integer-program
  solvers, `core/shown_counts.py`, `core/answer_sets.py`);
- withholding means, SDs, medians and shares of columns that "behave like categories"
  (`grouping.behaves_like_categories`, `table_min_cell`, `_share_withheld`);
- refusing logistic models with a small level or level-by-outcome cell
  (`predictor_model_refusal`) and table statistics (chi-square, Fisher, log-rank, correlations of
  coded columns) with a small cell (`table_statistic_refusal`);
- checking counts written into data notes against the answer (`counted_note`'s `rows`,
  `_note_count_withheld`), and hiding quality-report counts ("hidden", status "review", tiny
  tables reporting no counts at all, one bad-value check per column).

The person reading the answer already holds every row. Hiding a count from them on their own
screen protects no one: they can open the file. It does make the tool fail at its main job.
Basic tabulations came back as "not shown", Fisher's exact test (the right test for small
cells) was never shown, an n of 187 became "n hidden" because 9 rows had no value, and the
golden suites carried a whole grade ("hidden") for answers that were withheld rather than right.

The real privacy boundary is the machine. What must be protected is what leaves it: an exported
file, a results packet handed to a hosted model, a log or report that is published.

## Decision

1. **On screen, every answer shows its true numbers, small counts included.** Nothing is
   withheld, folded, rounded or replaced for small-cell or derivability reasons, on the question
   path, the governed SQL path, data notes, upload notes or the quality report.

2. **What leaves the machine is masked per cell, in one function.** Anything written out of the
   app as a file or a packet (an export, the planned results packet for a hosted model, a results
   file, a published log) goes through `core/export_boundary.py`:
   - `export_answer(presentation)` returns the claim and table with every count from 1 to
     `SMALL_CELL_THRESHOLD - 1` written as `"<11"`, how many were masked, and a visible note
     (`EXPORT_NOTE`) that says so;
   - `export_text(text)` does the same for one line of text.

   It is plain per-cell masking: each count is judged on its own. Nothing is hidden to stop a
   masked count being worked out from the rest, and no state is kept between exports. In a table,
   every whole number (never a bool) from 1 to 10 is masked, since the app's tables hold counts
   as integers and every other statistic as a float. In a sentence, every standalone whole number
   from 1 to 10 is masked, since a sentence cannot say which of its numbers are counts; a label
   digit ("Type 2") is masked too, which is the safe direction at this boundary. An unrecognised
   table shape is dropped, and the note says so.

   The threshold stays `small_cell_threshold` (11) in `nl_query.yaml`; it now configures this
   boundary only.

3. **Unchanged:**
   - patient data never leaves the machine: no network egress, a local model, the API on
     loopback only;
   - declared public estimates are shown as published ("trust the source", ADR014 §7), and
     cells the source suppressed stay hidden and are never turned into numbers;
   - counting is correct: patients are counted as patients and rows as rows (grain declarations,
     distinct patient keys at visit grain, the governed SQL path's refusal of visit rows).

| `TERM_NAME_CLASH_MODEL_REFUSAL` | Kept, reworded, detected by duplicate term names | Two predictors with one term name make a wrong model |
| `table_statistic_refusal`, `SMALL_CELL_TEST_REFUSAL` | Deleted | Chi-square, Fisher, log-rank and correlations are computed and shown |
| `_share_withheld`, `_value_statistic_withheld`, `_counts_small` | Deleted | Withholding only |
| `counted_note` checks (`rows=`, `scope=`, `_note_count_withheld`, "some") | Deleted; `counted_note` kept | Notes write their count (or percent) as it is |
| Governed SQL 2^k suppression, `MAX_CONDITIONS`, `MAX_DISCRETE_LEVELS`, `WITHHELD` | Deleted; `sql/privacy.py` renamed `sql/answer.py` | Withholding only. The step-by-step count is kept, now one scan |
| Governed SQL patient-row check (visit rows refused, IDs or confirmation) | Kept | Counting correctness: counts are labelled patients |
| Governed SQL static shape (no HAVING, UNION, MIN/MAX, windows, patient listings; PHI coarsened in GROUP BY; allowed quantiles) | Kept; refusal wording no longer claims privacy where the reason was suppression | v1's answer shape. Widening it is a follow-up for the owner, not part of this change |
| Quality-report suppression (`COUNT_HIDDEN`, `HIDDEN_STATUS`, tiny tables, one bad-value check per column), `row_count_min` contract rule, `minCellSize` | Deleted | On-screen withholding |
| Spelling holds naming only spellings of 11+ rows | Changed: every spelling is named | On-screen withholding |

| `n_filtered_out` | Deleted | Carried only for suppression lines |
| Session time zone UTC on DuckDB connections | Kept, moved to `nl_query_config.SESSION_TIME_ZONE` | Answers do not depend on the host time zone |

## Acceptance grading

`eval/corpus/score.py` grades the answer as shown on screen. A small count shown on screen is
graded on its value like any other; it is never a LEAK, and withholding it is no longer "held
correctly": a withheld value where the oracle has one is WRONG, a missing row is WRONG, and an
"Other" fold row is an unexpected row. LEAK now means the export boundary failed: the answer as
`export_answer` would write it still shows an oracle cell of 1-10 patients as a number
(`find_export_leaks`).

The harness's own outputs are a different boundary and keep masking: `results.md` (printed in CI,
uploaded as an artifact and written to the job summary) runs every reason through
`export_text`, and `results.json` stores the claim and table through `export_answer`.

## Consequences

- Answers are complete: Fisher's exact test for sparse tables, means of coded columns, models
  with small levels, n and exclusions in every claim.
- The exported copy of an answer is not derivability-safe: a masked "<11" beside a total and the
  other groups can be worked out. That is the owner's choice of plain per-cell masking for files.
- The grammar of a count of 1 ("1 values") is not fixed in this change.
- There is no export endpoint in the app yet; the function is ready for the planned results
  packet and is wired into the acceptance harness's results files today.
